# TAYVORIQ ORIGINALS — ECHO 17

Status: PILOT / LAB ONLY
Target format: 9:16, 24 fps, 20–30 s per episode
Production rule: every visible shot is generated specifically for the episode. No stock footage, no reused B-roll, no slideshow motion.

## Premise
At exactly 02:17, isolated public-address systems begin transmitting sounds and voices from 17 minutes in the future. A night maintenance technician named Malik is the first person who realizes the warnings are aimed directly at him.

## Why this series
- Strong cinematic hook within 1–2 seconds.
- One recurring lead makes character continuity measurable.
- Limited locations keep identity/world consistency achievable.
- Mystery creates episode-to-episode cliffhangers.
- Visual storytelling works without readable on-screen text.
- Every episode can be produced as an original mini-film.

## World Bible
Setting: near-future European city, grounded realism, no fantasy design language.
Primary location: underground metro maintenance tunnel, wet concrete, steel conduits, emergency lamps, puddles, sparse industrial signage.
Time: 02:17 at night.
Lighting: cold cyan practical lights, occasional warm sodium spill, red emergency lights only when the anomaly escalates.
Camera: cinematic handheld/dolly hybrid, controlled motion, 35–50 mm equivalent, shallow depth of field only on close shots.
Texture: photorealistic, wet surfaces, realistic skin and fabric, subtle filmic contrast, no artificial glossy CGI look.
Audio: tunnel ambience, electrical hum, distant train resonance, footsteps, PA crackle, restrained cinematic bass pulse.

## Character Lock — Malik
Male, early 30s, Mediterranean appearance, short dark wet hair, light stubble, tired but alert face, charcoal waterproof work jacket with subtle reflective piping, black work trousers, dark safety boots, compact red tool case.
Rule: same facial structure, haircut, stubble, body proportions, jacket, trousers, boots and tool case in every ECHO 17 shot unless the story explicitly changes them.

## Continuity Rules
1. Generate one approved identity/world reference before episode rendering.
2. Shot N+1 must use reference material from the approved world and prior accepted shot where the engine supports it.
3. Never regenerate the protagonist from pure text after identity lock unless continuity QA explicitly rejects the chain.
4. Keep screen direction and spatial geography stable.
5. Only repair the failed shot, never restart accepted shots.
6. Generated video must contain real subject/environment motion; camera-only motion does not pass.

## Pilot Episode 01 — “NICHT UMDREHEN”
Target: 25 s, 5 shots.

Narration/dialogue:
“Um zwei Uhr siebzehn war der Tunnel längst leer. Dann knackte der Lautsprecher. Eine Stimme sagte: ‘Wenn du das hörst, dreh dich nicht um.’ Malik erstarrte. Es war seine eigene Stimme. Hinter ihm gingen die Lichter aus. Eins nach dem anderen.”

Shot 01 — 0–5 s
Wide vertical establishing shot. Malik walks alone through the wet maintenance tunnel carrying the red tool case. Real footsteps, jacket movement, reflections and subtle breathing. Slow controlled camera retreat.

Shot 02 — 5–10 s
Medium shot. Ceiling PA speaker crackles. Malik stops mid-step and slowly looks upward, genuinely reacting to the sound. Camera moves closer, not a static zoom.

Shot 03 — 10–15 s
Close/medium profile. The warning is heard. Malik’s face changes from confusion to fear. His fingers tighten around the tool-case handle. Natural eye and head motion.

Shot 04 — 15–20 s
Over-shoulder shot looking down the tunnel behind Malik. Practical lights switch off one by one from the far end toward him. Malik remains foreground and physically turns his shoulders only slightly, resisting the urge to look back.

Shot 05 — 20–25 s
Tight frontal shot. The final light behind him dies. Red emergency glow flickers on. A fast moving shadow crosses the wet wall behind him while Malik hears a footstep and freezes. Cut to black on the sound.

End hook: no explanation; immediate reason to watch episode 02.

## Pilot Episode 02 — “TÜR 17”
Target: 27 s, 5 shots.

Narration/dialogue:
“Malik rannte bis zur Wartungstür siebzehn. Sie war seit Jahren versiegelt. Doch heute stand sie offen. Dahinter lag derselbe Tunnel. Dieselbe Pfütze. Derselbe rote Werkzeugkoffer. Nur Malik selbst fehlte. Dann kamen Schritte von hinten.”

Shot 01 — 0–5 s
Direct continuation. Malik runs through the same tunnel, realistic full-body stride and camera pursuit. Red emergency light recedes behind him.

Shot 02 — 5–10 s
He reaches a heavy industrial maintenance door and braces one hand against it, breathing hard. The door is already slightly open and moves a few centimeters by itself.

Shot 03 — 10–16 s
Malik pushes the door open and steps through. Reveal: an eerily identical parallel stretch of the same tunnel, same puddle geometry and lighting, but silent and empty. Camera follows across the threshold.

Shot 04 — 16–22 s
Malik sees an identical red tool case lying alone several meters ahead. He slowly approaches, crouches and touches it. His own case is still in his other hand. Keep both cases clearly visible without surreal morphing.

Shot 05 — 22–27 s
A distinct pair of footsteps sounds behind him. Malik rises and turns his head halfway. Camera stays on his face while an out-of-focus human silhouette appears deep behind him. Cut before identity is revealed.

## Pilot Quality Gate
PASS only if:
- Malik is recognizably the same person across both episodes.
- clothing/tool case/location continuity is stable.
- people actually walk/run/turn/crouch/react.
- no shot is merely a still image with camera movement.
- no unrelated footage is inserted.
- no visible text artifacts or generated subtitles.
- no grotesque anatomy, identity swap or object melting.
- each shot contributes directly to the narrative.
